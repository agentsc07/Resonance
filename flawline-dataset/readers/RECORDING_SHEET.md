# Reader recording sheet (slang / age / accent coverage)

Why: public corpora give clean fluent speech (VCTK) or speech with natural errors (Svarah), but no slang and no older clean speakers. The spec's answer is real readers who read a script we control, so the reference text is exact and slang is genuine.

## Protocol (spec)
- Phone about 30 cm away, voice-memo app, send the ORIGINAL file (not WhatsApp: it compresses audio).
- Start with 3 s of silence (room-noise sample).
- Read each script once, naturally, in your own way of speaking. Slang and dialect words are meant to be said as you would say them.
- Re-record freely if you stumble; we want a clean take.
- Sign the consent line below before recording.

### Script B01: The Lighthouse Keeper  (neutral, United States)

> The lighthouse stood at the edge of the world, or so the villagers believed. Every evening, before the fog
> rolled in, the keeper climbed the two hundred and twelve steps to light the lamp. Nobody thanked him; nobody
> noticed, until the night he was late. That night, three fishing boats drifted toward the rocks in total
> darkness, and the whole harbour learned what a small light can hold back. When he finally arrived, breathless
> and apologetic, no one scolded him. They simply stood in the cold and watched the beam sweep over the water,
> steady and unhurried, like a promise being kept. Some duties are invisible only because they are performed so
> faithfully. Remember that, the next time the dark seems quiet.

### Script B02: Not Yet Begun  (formal, United Kingdom)

> Friends, countrymen, fellow citizens: we meet at an hour when doubt is cheap and courage is dear. It is easy
> to be eloquent about success; it is far harder to be steadfast in adversity. Yet history does not remember
> those who hesitated at the threshold. It remembers the indefatigable, the stubborn, the ones who rose each
> morning and resumed the work as though yesterday's defeat had been a mere rehearsal. Let us not mistake
> difficulty for destiny. The road is long, the weather is foul, and the hour is late; but we are here, and we
> are many, and we have not yet begun to give our best. Therefore let us go forward, with resolve in our hearts
> and sovereignty over our own fear.

### Script B04: The Water in Our Taps  (formal, India (metro))

> Honourable members and fellow citizens, I rise today to speak about something we all use and rarely question:
> the water in our taps. In nearly every district, a child still walks two kilometres for a single bucket. Think
> about that. Think about the hours lost, the schooling forgone, the potential quietly drained away. We have the
> engineers. We have the funds. What we lack is urgency. I am not asking for charity; I am asking for
> accountability. Let us commit, this very year, to a plan with deadlines, with budgets, and with names attached
> to every promise. Because a nation that cannot quench its own thirst cannot claim to be building a future.

### Script B05: One Cup of Filter Coffee  (colloquial, India (Tamil Nadu))

Slang/dialect terms: what to say, no, phones only, isn't it, WhatsApp, thirty years back

> Good evening everyone, I am very happy to see so many of you here today, what to say. When I first came to
> this colony thirty years back, there was only one tea shop and one bus, and both were always late. But people
> were good, no? If somebody fell sick, the whole street would come with food and medicine. Now everyone is busy
> with their phones only. I am not saying phones are bad, I also use WhatsApp daily. Only thing is, let us not
> forget to knock on the neighbour's door. A small chat, one cup of filter coffee, that itself is enough to keep
> a community alive, isn't it?

### Script B06: The Last of the Storytellers  (formal, Ireland)

Slang/dialect terms: so we have, sure look, sure

> Now, I've lived long enough to know that a story told once is a story half-lost. My grandmother had a tale for
> every field and every hedgerow, and she'd tell them by the fire, slow and sure, as if the words were embers
> she was afraid to scatter. We've gone and swapped the fire for a screen, so we have. And I'm not scolding
> anybody, sure look, we all do it. But I'm asking you tonight to sit with the old people, to ask them the
> question you think you already know the answer to. Because when the last of them is gone, so is the music, and
> no cacophony of modern noise will ever fill that silence.

### Script B07: Sit a Spell  (colloquial, United States (South))

Slang/dialect terms: reckon, ain't, fixin' to, y'all, sit a spell, granddaddy, 'cause, folks

> Well now, I reckon every porch has a story, and ours was no different. My granddaddy used to sit out there
> every evening, whittling on a piece of cedar, and he'd say, son, ain't nothing in this world worth hurrying
> for except supper. We thought he was just an old man talking. Turns out he was fixin' to teach us something.
> The corn don't grow faster 'cause you stare at it, and folks don't heal quicker 'cause you fuss. Y'all keep
> that in mind when the world starts pushing. Sit a spell. Breathe. The good stuff, it comes round in its own
> sweet time.
