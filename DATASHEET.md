# Datasheet: Flawline

Contrastive speech-analytics dataset for hackathon Track C. Version 0.7 (7 Oct 2026). Format follows "Datasheets for Datasets" (Gebru et al.).

## Motivation
- **Purpose.** Test whether a system can find *delivery* flaws in speech (pace, pauses, intonation, volume, fluency, clarity, text fidelity), say where they are, and score them. Real recorded speakers read a known text, then controlled flaws are injected at known places and strengths, so every label is ground truth by construction.
- **Not for.** Speaker identification, accent classification, or judging any real person's speaking ability. The flaws are synthetic edits of other people's recordings.
- **Created by.** The Flawline team for the hackathon; labels are generated by `flawline-dataset/generator/`.

## Composition
- **564 clips, 9.97 hours**, FLAC, 22.05 kHz mono, 60–66 s each. 10 baseline recordings ("champion takes") plus derived clips.

- **Where (recording conditions).** Clean (C0), and synthetic: N20 (20 dB reversed multi-talker babble), N10 (10 dB pink noise), RVB (RT60 0.6 s reverb), PHN (300–3400 Hz phone band), MP3 (64 kbps), GAIN (±6 dB). 99 condition clips.
- **What (flaws).** 15 flaws in 7 categories: Pacing (PACE_FAST, PACE_SLOW), Pausing (PAUSE_BAD, PAUSE_LOST), Intonation (MONOTONE, UPTALK, EMPH_FLAT), Volume (FADE, SHOUT), Fluency (FILLER, REPEAT, RARE_HESIT), Clarity (SLUR), Text fidelity (WORD_SKIP, WORD_SWAP). Five levels each (L1 mild to L5 egregious). 444 single-flaw clips, 50 multi-flaw clips (2–3 flaws each at level 3), 70 clean or condition-only clips.
- **Labels.** One JSON per clip (`schema/label.schema.json`, v1.1.0): for every injected flaw its time span, category, level, the exact parameters used, the measured evidence, and a one-line cause. Word indices refer to the baseline transcript.
- **Splits.** `test` = B05, B08 (61 clips; whole speakers held out, never used to fit or select anything until the final run), `dev` = B03 (32), `train` = B01, B02, B04, B06, B07 (297), `extra` = B09, B10 (174; never used to fit or select anything).
- **Known gaps.** No speakers over 45, no slang or spontaneous speech, no children, no non-English. Eight read-aloud studio voices (VCTK) plus one 1962 open-air oratory voice: this is a small sample of speakers.

## Collection and generation
- **Baselines.** VCTK 0.92 first utterances (the elicitation paragraph and the Rainbow Passage), silence trimmed to 0.3 s margins, resampled, one gain change. Selected for being clean read speech. JFK excerpts cut from Rice University's copy of the address on the Internet Archive.
- **Word timings.** faster-whisper base.en matched to the known text, then snapped to energy valleys and glottal epochs so cuts fall at quiet points.
- **Flaw injection.** Placement from the transcript's grammar (spaCy): pauses and hesitations at clause boundaries, not inside phrases. Edits use the speaker's own audio only: pitch-synchronous time and pitch changes (PSOLA, Praat), the speaker's own room tone for gaps, own-voice schwa for fillers. An artifact gate rejects any edit whose splice click exceeds 6 dB over the recording's noise floor more than 5% of the time (never above 12 dB). Levels are nested (the L2 edit contains the L1 edit's anchor).
- **Reproducibility.** Seeded; the same takes regenerate bit-identically on the same platform. In a clean Linux/arm64 container 75 of 80 B01 clips matched the macOS build bit for bit and the other 5 differed by one 16-bit step at most.

## Quality checks (measured)
- `generator/qa.py`: no clipping, no zero-runs, schema valid, boundary checks on all clips.
- Dose-response: flaw strength grows with level on 8 of 8 VCTK speakers for 12 of 15 flaws; UPTALK on 7 of 8, REPEAT on 4 of 8 (tied levels) and EMPH_FLAT on 2 of 8.
- **Leakage audit** (can a classifier find the flaw from editing artifacts alone): 0.591 AUC on held-out test windows (pass mark 0.60), 0.612 leave-one-speaker-out. Per flaw about 0.7 for RARE_HESIT, FILLER, PAUSE_BAD and EMPH_FLAT. The dataset still carries detectable editing traces for those flaws.
- **Not done:** the by-ear pilot gate (realism rating of each flaw by human listeners) has not been run. No human panel. Whether the flaws sound natural has not been verified by listening.

## Human reference set
`flawline-dataset/human/` holds a small set of readings listened to by people: `clips/` (neutral file names; unedited readings plus five dataset clips with hidden injected flaws at levels 3 to 5), `labels_<rater>.json` (per clip: marks with time and area, an overall 1 to 10 score, an optional note, timestamp), `hidden_map.json` (which clips carry injected flaws; never shown to raters), `transcripts.json` (the read-aloud text of each clip) and `review_<reviewer>.json` (agree / disagree / unsure on engine flags that no listener marked). Raters entered a name or alias of their choice. Only recordings whose speakers consented are included; the VCTK and JFK clips keep their own licences. It is a small reference for checking the engine against listeners, not a rating of any speaker.

## Uses and limits
- Suited to: benchmarking detectors of delivery flaws against known truth; invariance tests (does a noisy recording look flawed?); score-calibration experiments.
- Not suited to: claims about how humans rate speakers; accent or demographic analysis (one or two speakers per accent).
- The JFK baselines are a historical open-air recording with rhetorical pauses and crowd noise; the audit flags both excerpts as not clean (B10 possibly contains hesitation sounds, unconfirmed by ear).
- Altered clips are derivative works. A recognisable public figure's voice (B09, B10) appears in edited form; the edits are mechanical and labelled.

## Distribution and licensing
Each subset keeps its own licence (`flawline-dataset/LICENSES.md`): VCTK clips CC BY 4.0 (attribution to CSTR, University of Edinburgh); JFK clips public domain. The generator and engine code are released with the repository. Clips are distributed as a zip with SHA-256 checksums (`checksums.sha256`); the released package must include `takes/` (baseline audio and alignment JSON) because re-aligning on another machine moves word timings by up to 0.37 s.
